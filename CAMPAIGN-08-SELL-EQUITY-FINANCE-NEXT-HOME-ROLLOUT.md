## CORE POSITIONING

This campaign is for homeowners who are not simply selling a house; they are trying to coordinate a sale, access equity, understand financing, and move into the next home with as little friction as possible.

The strategic sequence is:
1. Understand the current home's likely market position and estimated equity.
2. Build the selling strategy.
3. Determine how sale proceeds may affect cash-to-close and reserves.
4. Review financing for the next home.
5. Coordinate timing between sale, financing, offer strategy, and move.

Do not promise a specific sales price, equity amount, mortgage approval, rate, payment, closing date, or simultaneous closing outcome.

## EDUCATIONAL POST — EQUITY IS NOT THE SAME AS CASH

**Your home may have equity — but that does not mean every dollar of equity becomes cash at closing.**

A useful seller strategy starts by estimating:

- likely market value
- mortgage payoff
- selling expenses
- negotiated concessions, if any
- taxes, HOA or other transaction items when applicable
- estimated net proceeds

Those net proceeds may then affect the financing plan for your next home.

The goal is not simply to ask, “How much is my house worth?”

The better question is:

**“What may I realistically have available for the next move?”**

---

## EDUCATIONAL POST — SELL FIRST OR BUY FIRST?

**Should you sell first or buy first?**

There is no universal answer.

The right strategy can depend on:

- your current mortgage
- available cash and reserves
- estimated equity
- debt-to-income qualification
- whether you need sale proceeds for the next purchase
- market conditions
- offer competitiveness
- temporary housing flexibility
- lender and underwriting requirements

Before writing offers, build the sequence.

---

## EDUCATIONAL POST — THE NEXT PAYMENT MATTERS

A homeowner can be ready to sell but still not know what the next home should cost.

Before shopping, review:

- estimated proceeds from the current sale
- target down payment
- desired reserves
- total monthly payment comfort zone
- taxes and insurance
- HOA dues where applicable
- mortgage insurance where applicable
- loan program and qualification

Your next-home search should be built around a payment and cash-to-close strategy — not just a sales price.

---

## EDUCATIONAL POST — TIMING

The hardest part of a sell-and-buy move is often not the house.

It is the timing.

A coordinated strategy may need to account for:

- listing preparation
- market time
- buyer due diligence
- appraisal and financing contingencies
- your own mortgage approval
- offer structure on the next home
- possession dates
- temporary occupancy or short-term housing when needed
- moving logistics

Build the timeline before the pressure starts.
