## Organic Educational Content

### Post 1 — Before You Visit the Model Home

Before walking into a builder's sales office, understand who represents whom and whether buyer-agent registration rules apply. Representation should be clear before you start negotiating price, incentives or contract terms.

CTA: Review My New Construction Plan.

### Post 2 — Builder Incentives Are Not Automatically Free Money

A large builder incentive can still be a poor deal if it requires a financing structure that costs more overall. Compare rate, points, lender fees, closing-cost credits, buydowns, price and long-term financing impact together.

CTA: Compare the full transaction.

### Post 3 — Design-Center Deposits Can Change Your Numbers

Upgrades can increase the contract price, required deposits and final cash needed. Some deposits may or may not count toward funds needed at closing depending on the transaction. Plan design choices around the financing structure, not only the showroom presentation.

CTA: Build the numbers before the upgrades.

### Post 4 — Do You Still Need an Inspection on New Construction?

New does not mean flawless. Buyers may consider inspections at appropriate construction stages and before closing, subject to contract rights, builder access and local practices.

CTA: Build your milestone checklist early.

## Compliance / Accuracy Guardrails

Do not state or imply:
- builder incentives are guaranteed
- builder incentives are always the best financial option
- a preferred builder lender is always better or worse
- rates, payments or closing costs are guaranteed
- approval is guaranteed
- every upgrade adds equivalent resale value
- appraisal will equal contract price
- every deposit will count toward down payment or cash to close
- every new-construction property or transaction qualifies for every loan program
- inspections are unnecessary because a home is new
- a buyer can always obtain representation after registering directly with a builder

Use language such as:
- incentives and terms vary by builder, lender, community and transaction
- financing is subject to qualification, underwriting and property requirements
- representation and builder registration rules may vary
- upgrade deposits, credits and closing costs depend on the contract and financing structure
- appraisal and property value are not guaranteed
- inspection rights depend on the contract and applicable rules
