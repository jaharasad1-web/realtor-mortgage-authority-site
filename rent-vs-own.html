What Is Renting Really Costing You?

Renting and owning both have real costs. The right decision depends on your finances, timeline, goals, available programs and the total monthly housing picture—not a slogan.

Renting vs. Owning: Compare the Full Picture

Renting

Rent may offer flexibility and fewer direct maintenance responsibilities, but rent can increase and generally does not create ownership equity for the tenant.

Consider: monthly rent, renewal increases, renter fees, flexibility, savings goals and how long you expect to stay.

Owning

Ownership can provide housing stability and the opportunity to build equity, while adding responsibilities such as taxes, insurance, maintenance and transaction costs.

Consider: total payment, down payment, closing costs, reserves, maintenance, mortgage insurance, HOA dues and your expected time in the home.

Why the Monthly Payment Alone Is Not Enough

Equity

A portion of mortgage payments may reduce principal and build equity over time. Property values can rise or fall, so appreciation is never guaranteed.

Rent Increases

Future rent is controlled by market conditions and your lease. Compare today's rent with what you may pay at renewal and over several years.

Ownership Costs

Taxes, insurance, maintenance, mortgage insurance, HOA dues and utilities all matter when comparing the true cost of owning.

Available Programs

FHA, Conventional, USDA, VA and eligible down-payment-assistance programs may create different paths depending on your qualifications and property.
