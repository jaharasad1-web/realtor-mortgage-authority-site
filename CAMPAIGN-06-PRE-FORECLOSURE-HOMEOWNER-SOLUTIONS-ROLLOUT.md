## POSITIONING

This campaign is education-first, confidential and option-centered. The objective is not to pressure a homeowner into selling. The objective is to help the homeowner understand timing, servicer options, equity, sale alternatives, transition planning and professional resources early enough to make an informed decision.

Core headline:

**You Have Options. Let’s Review Them Early.**

Core message:

If you are behind on your mortgage, received a notice, or are worried about foreclosure, the earlier you understand your choices, the more time you may have to act. The right path depends on your mortgage, servicer, equity, timeline, goals and eligibility.

The campaign may discuss loan modification, forbearance/deferment, traditional sale, short sale, investor/as-is sale and transition planning. No option is presented as universally best.

## FACEBOOK / INSTAGRAM EDUCATIONAL POST — SERVICER FIRST

Before making a major decision, homeowners who are behind on mortgage payments should contact their mortgage servicer promptly and ask what loss-mitigation options may be available.

Questions may include:

- Is a loan modification available?
- Is there a repayment plan, forbearance or deferment option?
- What deadlines apply?
- Has a foreclosure sale date been scheduled?
- What documents are required?

A real-estate strategy should be built around the actual timeline — not guesses.

If selling may become part of the solution, I can help you evaluate value, equity, timing and sale options while you continue communicating with the servicer.

## EDUCATIONAL POST — EQUITY

**Foreclosure pressure does not automatically mean you have no equity.**

Before choosing a path, estimate the likely market value, mortgage payoff, selling costs, liens and timing.

If there is meaningful equity, understanding the numbers early may help you compare a traditional sale, an as-is sale or other alternatives before the foreclosure timeline becomes more restrictive.

## EDUCATIONAL POST — SELL VS AS-IS

A fast as-is sale can be useful in some situations, but speed is only one factor.

Compare:

- expected sale price
- repairs or preparation required
- commissions and other costs
- closing timeline
- certainty of closing
- estimated net proceeds
- time remaining before a scheduled foreclosure event

The fastest offer is not automatically the best financial outcome. The highest price is not automatically the best solution either. The right answer depends on the homeowner’s priorities and timeline.

## COMPLIANCE / ETHICAL GUARDRAILS

Do not claim:

- foreclosure can definitely be stopped
- a modification will be approved
- a homeowner must sell
- an investor sale will produce the best outcome
- a particular net-proceeds amount without a property-specific analysis
- guaranteed debt forgiveness
- guaranteed short-sale approval
- guaranteed closing before a foreclosure date

Always:

- encourage prompt servicer contact
- state that availability depends on loan, servicer, timeline and eligibility
- distinguish real-estate information from legal/tax advice
- recommend HUD-approved housing counseling or qualified legal/tax advice when appropriate
- present a traditional sale, as-is sale and keep-home path as alternatives to compare, not predetermined outcomes
- preserve confidentiality and avoid exploitative urgency language
